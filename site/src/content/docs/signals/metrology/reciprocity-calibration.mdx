A laboratory standard microphone is calibrated without a reference
microphone. Two microphones are coupled acoustically, one drives as a sound
source and the other receives, and the electrical transfer impedance
$Z_{\mathrm{e},12} = U_2/i_1$ that is measured is the product of their two
sensitivities times the acoustic transfer impedance of whatever couples them,
which is computed:

$$
M_1 M_2 = \frac{Z_{\mathrm{e},12}}{Z_{\mathrm{a},12}}
$$

In a **coupler** (IEC 61094-2) $Z_{\mathrm{a},12}$ is the acoustic impedance
of a small enclosed volume of gas; in a **free field** (IEC 61094-3) it is
that of a spherical wave between the two acoustic centres. Three microphones
measured in pairs give three products, and each sensitivity follows from them
alone. The three modules of `metrology` that do this are
`reciprocity_coupler`, `reciprocity_free_field` and `reciprocity_calibration`,
which holds the result and the budget both share.

The readings on this page are synthetic: electrical transfer impedances built
from three sensitivities the page knows, so every step can be checked against
them.

## 1. How the measurement goes

Both parts use three microphones, two of which must be reciprocal, or two
microphones and an auxiliary sound source, one of which must be reciprocal
(5.1.1). Each pair is measured in turn: one microphone is driven by a current,
measured as the voltage across a calibrated impedance in series with it, and
the other's open-circuit voltage is read by the insert voltage technique
(5.3). The quotient is the electrical transfer impedance. In a coupler the two
face each other across a cavity of known shape; in a free field they face
each other on their principal axes in an anechoic room.

## 2. The microphones and the coupler

Each microphone enters the acoustic transfer impedance through its acoustic
impedance and its front cavity. Annex E writes the impedance as a lumped
compliance, mass and resistance, or equivalently as the equivalent volume at
low frequencies, the resonance frequency and the loss factor; E.4 states the
representation good to about 1,3 times the resonance frequency. The front
cavity's measured volume, depth and diameter come from E.2 and E.3. The
coupler is either a plane-wave coupler of the diameter of the front cavities
or a large-volume coupler, with the nominal dimensions of Tables C.1 and C.2
in `metrology.IEC61094_2_TABLE_C1` and `metrology.IEC61094_2_TABLE_C2`.
Figure C.2 draws each microphone of a large-volume coupler with its face a
distance F back from the cavity, through a bore of the front-cavity diameter
B: `LargeVolumeCoupler` takes that length as `port_length_m`, and the two
bores of the LS1P coupler add 2,4 % to its volume, a tenth of a decibel on
each sensitivity.

```python
import numpy as np
from phonometry import fluids, metrology

mics = tuple(
    metrology.ReciprocityMicrophone(
        equivalent_volume_m3=v,
        resonance_frequency_hz=f0,
        loss_factor=d,
        front_cavity_volume_m3=0.534e-6,   # measured, E.3
        front_cavity_depth_m=1.95e-3,      # D of Table C.1
        front_cavity_diameter_m=18.6e-3,   # B of Table C.1
    )
    for v, f0, d in ((148e-9, 8200.0, 1.05), (141e-9, 8350.0, 1.00), (152e-9, 8050.0, 1.10))
)
coupler = metrology.PlaneWaveCoupler(
    length_m=7.5e-3,
    diameter_m=18.6e-3,
    capillary=metrology.CapillaryTube(length_m=0.05, radius_m=0.2e-3, count=2),
)
conditions = {"temperature_c": 22.4, "static_pressure_pa": 100200.0, "relative_humidity_percent": 46.0}
f = metrology.exact_frequencies(20, 10000, fraction=3)
check = metrology.check_coupler(f, coupler, mics, **conditions)
print(check.passes, round(check.length_to_diameter_ratio, 3), check.ratio_recommended)   # True 0.613 True

large = metrology.LargeVolumeCoupler(length_m=12.55e-3, diameter_m=42.88e-3, port_length_m=0.80e-3)   # E, C, F of Table C.2
print(round(large.cavity_volume_m3 * 1e9), round(large.closed_volume_m3(mics[:2]) * 1e9))   # 18124 19626 (mm³)
```

`check_coupler` holds the coupler to what its formulas need: Formulas (A.3)
and (A.4) "are valid for the frequency range given by $\omega\rho a^2 >
100\eta$", 3 Hz and 12 Hz for the LS1P and LS2aP couplers of Table C.1; the
approximation (A.2) of a large-volume coupler to $0{,}125 < R < 8$ and
$X > 5$; and the conditions to the domain Annex F states for its air. Two
things are reported without entering the verdict: the ratio C.2 recommends,
0,5 to 0,75 between the distance of the diaphragms and the diameter, and a
frequency below 20 Hz with the approximation, where A.2 asks for the full
solution "or the corresponding uncertainty component shall be increased
accordingly" (`full_solution_advised`).

## 3. The acoustic transfer impedance and its corrections

In the plane-wave coupler, Formula (4) treats the whole system as a
transmission line of length $l_0$ between the diaphragms, the coupler plus
the two front-cavity depths, terminated at each end by the microphone's
acoustic impedance in parallel with the excess volume of its front cavity
(7.3.3.1):

$$
\frac{1}{Z'_{\mathrm{a},12}} = \frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{a},0}}\left[
\left(\frac{Z_{\mathrm{a},0}}{Z_{\mathrm{a},1}} + \frac{Z_{\mathrm{a},0}}{Z_{\mathrm{a},2}}\right)\cosh\gamma l_0
+ \left(1 + \frac{Z_{\mathrm{a},0}^2}{Z_{\mathrm{a},1} Z_{\mathrm{a},2}}\right)\sinh\gamma l_0\right]
\tag{4}
$$

The heat conduction and the viscous losses at the cylindrical wall enter
through the propagation coefficient and wave impedance of Formulas (A.3) and
(A.4), and the heat conduction at the two diaphragms through the admittance of
Formula (A.5). In a large-volume coupler the gas is a pure compliance,
Formula (3), and the heat conduction makes its volume look larger by the
complex factor of Formula (A.1),

$$
\Delta_\mathrm{H} = \frac{\kappa}{1 + (\kappa - 1)\,E_V},
\qquad
X = \frac{f\,l^2}{\kappa\,\alpha_t}
$$

with $E_V$ Gerber's temperature transfer function of the cylinder and $l$ its
volume to surface ratio. The capillary tubes that equalise the static
pressure shunt the cavity, and Formula (6) divides the transfer impedance by
$\Delta_\mathrm{C} = 1 + n Z''_{\mathrm{a},12}/Z_{\mathrm{a,C}}$, with the
input impedance of an open tube of Formulas (B.1) to (B.3).

```python
pairs = ((0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 0))
za = [metrology.coupler_transfer_impedance(f, coupler, mics[i], mics[j], **conditions) for i, j in pairs]
print(za[0].heat_conduction_correction_db[[1, 16, 25]].round(3))   # [-0.287 -0.051  0.004]
print(za[0].capillary_correction_db[[1, 16, 25]].round(3))         # [-0.213  0.011 -0.   ]
```

At 31,6 Hz the heat conduction lowers the transfer impedance by 0,29 dB and
the two tubes by another 0,21 dB; at 1 kHz both are a few hundredths. The two
annexes print tables meant for testing exactly this arithmetic.
`temperature_transfer_function` sums the full solution of the annex's
reference [A.1], which A.2 requires below 20 Hz unless the uncertainty
component is increased instead, and which gives every entry of
Table A.1 to the 0,000 01 the annex states for them; Formula (A.2) is the
approximation, accurate to 0,01 % in modulus for $X > 5$:

```python
print(metrology.temperature_transfer_function(1.0, 100.0).round(5))                          # (0.97179+0.02758j)
print(metrology.temperature_transfer_function(1.0, 100.0, method="approximation").round(5))  # (0.97179+0.02758j)
air = fluids.air(temperature_c=23.0, static_pressure_pa=101325.0, relative_humidity_percent=50.0)
tube = metrology.capillary_tube_impedance([20.0, 1000.0], length_m=0.05, radius_m=1 / 6000, gas=air)
print((tube.impedance_pa_s_m3 / 1e9).round(3))   # [3.015+0.097j 8.331+4.288j]
```

Table B.1 prints 3,015 and 8,331 GPa·s/m³ for this tube, Table B.2 0,097 and
4,287. The radius the table heads "0,1667 mm" is 1/6 mm: with 0,1667 mm the
values come out 0,08 % low, beyond the last printed figure (an erratum). Even
with 1/6 mm the air of Annex F does not give every entry to its last figure:
the 186 complex entries come back within 0,002 GPa·s/m³, the residue reaching
0,0011 in the real part and 0,0018 in the imaginary near the resonances of the
tubes, and 0,0005 and 0,0007 from 20 Hz to 250 Hz, as the 4,288 here shows.

## 4. The pressure sensitivities

Formula (2) gives the product of each pair, and Formula (7) the three
sensitivities from the three products:

$$
|M_{\mathrm{p},1}| = \left\{\left|\frac{Z_{\mathrm{e},12}Z_{\mathrm{e},31}}{Z_{\mathrm{e},23}}\right|
\left|\frac{Z''_{\mathrm{a},23}}{Z''_{\mathrm{a},12}Z''_{\mathrm{a},31}}\right|
\left|\frac{\Delta_\mathrm{C,12}\Delta_\mathrm{C,31}}{\Delta_\mathrm{C,23}}\right|\right\}^{1/2}
\tag{7}
$$

```python
true = [m0 * m.complex_equivalent_volume_m3(f) / m.equivalent_volume_m3 for m0, m in zip((0.0496, 0.0473, 0.0512), mics)]
ze = [true[i] * true[j] * z.transfer_impedance_pa_s_m3 for (i, j), z in zip(pairs, za)]   # Formula (2)

c = metrology.pressure_reciprocity(f, ze, za)
print(c.sensitivity_level_db[:, 16].round(2))    # [-26.03 -26.44 -25.76]
print(c.sensitivity_mv_per_pa[:, 16].round(2))   # [49.93 47.64 51.51]
print(np.abs(c.sensitivity_v_per_pa - true).max() < 1e-15)   # True
```

The phase comes from the same products: the complex sensitivity is solved, not
only its modulus. A reciprocity measurement fixes products, and a product does
not change when every sensitivity changes sign together, so the sign of the
square root is a convention. The library takes the branch that is continuous
in frequency with a non-negative real part at the lowest frequency, and
`phase_deg` refers the phase to 0 to 360° as 5.7.1 of both parts asks. With two
microphones and an auxiliary source, the ratio of their sensitivities measured
against the source replaces the third pair, Formula (8):

```python
pair = metrology.pressure_reciprocity_pair(f, ze[0], za[0], true[0] / true[1])
print(pair.sensitivity_level_db[:, 16].round(2))   # [-26.03 -26.44]
```

## 5. A free-field calibration

In a free field the transmitter is, seen from far enough, a simple source at
its acoustic centre. The product of Formula (7) of IEC 61094-3 carries the
distance $d_{12}$ between the acoustic centres, the density of the air, the
phase of the spherical wave and its attenuation over the distance
$d_{\mathrm{m}12}$ the wave travels between the diaphragms:

$$
M_{\mathrm{f},1} M_{\mathrm{f},2} = -\mathrm{j}\,\frac{2 d_{12}}{\rho f}\,
\frac{U_2}{i_1}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}k d_{12}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\alpha d_{\mathrm{m}12}}
\tag{7}
$$

The attenuation is computed by the five steps of Annex B, ISO 9613-1 adjusted
to the quantities of IEC 61094-2, and the wave number takes the speed of
sound of IEC 61094-2 Annex F with the dispersion of its NOTE to F.3, which
Annex B.1 points to.

```python
ff = metrology.exact_frequencies(1000, 40000, fraction=3)
ffc = {"temperature_c": 23.1, "static_pressure_pa": 101100.0, "relative_humidity_percent": 52.0}
att = metrology.reciprocity_air_attenuation(ff, **ffc)
print(att.attenuation_db_per_m[[0, 10, 13, 15]].round(4))   # [0.0053 0.1348 0.4713 0.9877]
```

Three LS2aP microphones 0,25 m apart, with acoustic centres a few millimetres
in front of their diaphragms that recede towards the diaphragm at high
frequency (Annex A shows such curves; these are illustrative):

```python
ratio = ff / 23000.0
true_ff = [m0 / (1.0 - ratio**2 + 1.3j * ratio) for m0 in (0.0128, 0.0124, 0.0131)]
centre = 4.5e-3 / (1.0 + (ff / 30000.0) ** 2)
ze_ff = [
    metrology.free_field_transfer_impedance(
        ff, true_ff[i], true_ff[j], diaphragm_distance_m=0.25, acoustic_centres_m=(centre, centre), **ffc
    )
    for i, j in pairs
]   # Formula (D.1)
cf = metrology.free_field_reciprocity(
    ff, ze_ff, diaphragm_distances_m=(0.25, 0.25, 0.25), acoustic_centres_m=(centre, centre, centre), **ffc
)
print(cf.sensitivity_level_db[:, 10].round(2))   # [-37.76 -38.03 -37.55]
print(np.abs(cf.sensitivity_v_per_pa - true_ff).max() < 1e-15)   # True
```

Formula (8) as printed omits the factor $-\mathrm{j}$ that it inherits from
the three products of Formula (7), and Formula (9), which the corrigendum
makes the complex sensitivity, omits it too. The modulus is unaffected; the
phase of the printed formulas is 45° off at every frequency, and Formula (D.1)
keeps the factor. The library follows Formula (7) (an erratum).

## 6. The uncertainty budgets

Both parts list the components of a calibration in their Table 1
(`IEC61094_2_TABLE_1`, `IEC61094_3_TABLE_1`) and ask for each as a standard
uncertainty as a function of frequency. The components of the acoustic
transfer impedance are found, as both describe, "by repeating a calculation
while the various components are changed one at a time by their associated
uncertainty": `coupler_parameter_uncertainty` and
`free_field_parameter_uncertainty` do that, each microphone's parameter moved
in turn and the three changes combined into the one row of the table. The
standard uncertainties they move travel together in a
`CouplerInputUncertainties` or a `FreeFieldInputUncertainties`, one keyword
per input quantity, and one left at zero adds no component.

```python
pu = metrology.coupler_parameter_uncertainty(
    f, coupler, mics,
    metrology.CouplerInputUncertainties(
        u_coupler_length_m=3e-6, u_coupler_diameter_m=3e-6, u_capillary_radius_m=2e-6,
        u_static_pressure_pa=10.0, u_temperature_k=0.05, u_relative_humidity_percent=2.0,
        u_front_cavity_depth_m=3e-6, u_front_cavity_volume_m3=1e-9, u_equivalent_volume_m3=1e-9,
        u_resonance_frequency_hz=50.0, u_loss_factor=0.03,
    ),
    **conditions,
)
print(pu.components_db["capillary_tube_dimensions"][[1, 16]].round(4))   # [0.0086 0.0002]

x = f / 10000
b = metrology.reciprocity_uncertainty_budget(
    f,
    {
        **pu.components_db,
        "series_impedance": 0.002, "voltage_ratio": 0.003, "cross_talk": 0.001 + 0.004 * x**2,
        "polarizing_voltage": 0.0025, "radial_wave_motion": 0.03 * x**2,
        "heat_conduction_theory": 0.002, "repeatability": 0.004, "rounding": 0.0006,
    },
)
print(b.expanded_uncertainty_db[[1, 16, 25]].round(3))   # [0.023 0.015 0.041]

pf = metrology.free_field_parameter_uncertainty(
    ff,
    metrology.FreeFieldInputUncertainties(
        u_distance_m=0.1e-3, u_acoustic_centre_m=0.5e-3, u_static_pressure_pa=20.0, u_temperature_k=0.1,
        u_relative_humidity_percent=2.0, u_air_attenuation_ratio=0.1 / np.sqrt(3.0),
    ),
    diaphragm_distances_m=(0.25, 0.25, 0.25), acoustic_centres_m=(centre, centre, centre), **ffc,
)
xf = ff / 40000
bf = metrology.reciprocity_uncertainty_budget(
    ff,
    {
        **pf.components_db,
        "series_impedance": 0.002, "voltage_ratio": 0.003, "cross_talk": 0.002 + 0.01 * xf,
        "noise": 0.005, "reflections": 0.01 + 0.02 * xf, "polarizing_voltage": 0.0025, "repeatability": 0.01,
    },
    field="free_field",
)
print(bf.expanded_uncertainty_db[[0, 10, 15]].round(3))   # [0.049 0.054 0.072]
```

The expanded uncertainty is $k = 2$ times the root-sum-square, which IEC
61094-2 7.5 states; IEC 61094-3 7.8 asks for the factor of a 95 % coverage
probability, which `coverage_factor` sets when a budget is dominated by a
component with few degrees of freedom. The free-field budget moves each
acoustic centre by 0,5 mm in turn, and the attenuation by the ±10 % B.2
states, taken as a rectangular distribution. The components on this page are
illustrative.
Passed as `expanded_uncertainty_db`, the budget draws the band of the
calibration's plot, and its legend states how wide the band is.

**IEC 61094-2:2009:** the products of Formula (2) and the sensitivities of
Formulas (7) and (8), complex; the acoustic transfer impedance of a

Formula (6) and Annex B; the lumped microphone impedance and Formula (E.1) of
Annex E; Tables C.1, C.2 and C.3 as published data, the wave-motion
correction of Table C.3 with the frequency scaling of C.3 for hydrogen; the
check of what the formulas require (A.2, A.3, Annex F); Table 1 and the
one-at-a-time recalculation of 7.5. Table A.1 is reproduced to its stated
0,000 01; Tables B.1 and B.2 to within 0,002 GPa·s/m³, more than their
printed half-unit near the resonances of the tubes (section 3).
**IEC 61094-3:2016 with COR1:2016:** the products of Formula (7), the
sensitivities of Formulas (8) and (9) with the factor of (7), Formula (D.1);
the attenuation of Annex B and the speed of sound with the dispersion of IEC
61094-2 F.3; the acoustic centre of 6.5; the arrangement of 6.4 and 7.3;
Table 1 and the recalculation of 7.8. Table B.1 is reproduced by ISO 9613-1,
as its text says it was computed.
</ScopeClaim>

<ScopeClaim status="covered" label="Covered, by a reading of the text">
**The branch of the square root.** IEC 61094-2 asks for the phase "by a
similar procedure from the phase angle of each term" and IEC 61094-3 derives
it from Formula (8), both referred to 0 to 2π; the library takes the branch
continuous in frequency with a non-negative real part at the lowest
frequency, and fixes the other two microphones by the products. **Dimension F
of Table C.2** is the length of the bore that Figure C.2 draws, of the
front-cavity diameter B, between each microphone face and the cavity; its
volume and its wall join those of the coupler. **The surface of a
large-volume coupler** closed by microphones is that of the closed cylinder
plus the wall of each bore, of each front cavity and of its thread, the
diaphragm taking the place of the port it closes (7.3.2.2). **The ratio of
C.2** is read on the distance between the diaphragms, which 5.4 and C.2 call
the length of the coupler.
</ScopeClaim>
