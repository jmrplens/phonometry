A sound level meter in service goes back to a laboratory every year or two
for its **periodic tests**, the short list of key checks IEC 61672-3:2013
keeps "to the minimum considered necessary" (Clause 1): a calibrator on the
microphone, the frequency weightings acoustically at three frequencies and
electrically at the octaves, the weightings and time weightings at 1 kHz, the
stability over half an hour and at the top of the range, the level linearity,
the tonebursts, the C-weighted peak and the overload indicator. The laboratory
measures; `metrology.verify_sound_level_meter_periodic` grades what it
measured, clause by clause, and writes the statement Clause 22 prescribes for
the outcome. It runs none of the tests.

This page grades the record of a synthetic class 1 meter whose one fault is a
0.25 ms toneburst, then shows what the verdict needs from a record to call
it complete.

## 1. The rule every clause is graded by

IEC 61672-3 grades a result the way every IEC TC 29 instrument standard
written since 2013 does (4.1): the measured deviation from the design goal
within the acceptance limits of IEC 61672-1 **and** the laboratory's actual
expanded uncertainty, for a coverage probability of 95 %, within the maximum
permitted by Table B.1 of IEC 61672-1, both limits inclusive. The
uncertainty is not added to the deviation; the tolerance the meter has to
meet lies beyond the acceptance limit by that maximum. Each result is one
`metrology.verify_conformance`, and [Compliance and
verification](/phonometry/signals/metrology/compliance-verification/) shows
the rule on the printed examples of IEC 61672-1 Table C.1.

A result measured with more uncertainty than its maximum "shall not be used
to evaluate conformance" (4.3). It is not a failure of the meter, and the
verdict does not pass while it is there: the verdict lists it under
`unusable`, apart from the results under `failed`.

The one exception is 4.4. The acoustical test of the frequency weighting
(Clause 12) and the electrical one (Clause 13) correct the indications with
the manufacturer's free-field or random-incidence correction data, and the
uncertainty of that data is part of the laboratory's budget. Without it, the
laboratory's uncertainty "shall not exceed" the maximum; when the total
exceeds the maximum only because of it, "testing may proceed", and the
result is one that did not conform, with that reason in the statement. The
record takes the uncertainty without the correction data beside the total
(section 6 shows one); without it, the total is all the verdict knows and 4.3
applies.

## 2. What each clause is judged against

| Clause | Test | Acceptance limits (IEC 61672-1) | Maximum uncertainty (Table B.1) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 12 | Frequency weighting with acoustical signals, at 125 Hz and 8 kHz relative to 1 kHz | Table 3 | 0.60 dB up to 4 kHz, 0.70 dB above |
| 13 | Every frequency weighting with electrical signals, 63 Hz to 16 kHz (class 1) or to 8 kHz (class 2) | Table 3 | 0.60 dB, 0.70 dB above 4 kHz, 1.00 dB above 10 kHz |
| 14.2 | C and Z against A at 1 kHz | ±0.2 dB (5.5.9) | 0.20 dB |
| 14.3 | S and time-averaged against F at 1 kHz | ±0.1 dB (5.8.3) | 0.20 dB |
| 15 | Long-term stability, 25 min to 35 min | ±0.1 dB, class 2 ±0.3 dB (5.14.2) | 0.10 dB |
| 16 | Level linearity at 8 kHz on the reference level range | ±0.8 dB, class 2 ±1.1 dB (5.6.5) | 0.30 dB |
| 17 | Level linearity including the level range control, at 1 kHz | ±0.8 dB, class 2 ±1.1 dB (5.6.5) | 0.30 dB |
| 18 | 4 kHz tonebursts of 200 ms, 2 ms and 0.25 ms | Table 4 | 0.30 dB |
| 19 | C-weighted peak: one cycle at 8 kHz, half cycles at 500 Hz | Table 5 | 0.35 dB |
| 20 | Overload indication, positive against negative half cycle | 1.5 dB (5.11.3) | 0.25 dB |
| 21 | High-level stability, 5 min near the top of the least-sensitive range | ±0.1 dB, class 2 ±0.3 dB (5.15.2) | 0.10 dB |

Two things in the table are as the pages print them and not what one might
expect. The maximum uncertainty of 14.3, 0.20 dB, is twice its acceptance
limit, 0.1 dB. And Part 3 grades the level linearity of Clauses 16 and 17
against 5.6.5 alone: the limit of 5.6.6 on a 1 dB to 10 dB change in level is
a pattern-evaluation requirement (IEC 61672-2, 9.8.1.7) that Part 3 never
mentions.

Clauses 10 and 11 are records, not tests: the indications at the calibration
check frequency before and after the adjustment, and the self-generated
noise, which "is reported for information only and is not used to assess
conformance" and without an uncertainty (11.1.2, NOTE 2). Clauses 16, 19 and
20 each hold a yes/no requirement besides the numbers: no overload or
under-range indication inside the linear operating range the manual states
(16.4), the C-weighted peak signals shall not cause an overload indication
(19.3, 19.5), and the overload indicator shall latch on (20.5).

## 3. Grading a record

`metrology.SoundLevelMeterPeriodicMeasurements` holds what the laboratory
measured, each graded result beside its expanded uncertainty and every field
named by its unit. The toneburst responses and the C-weighted peak
differences go in as measured, relative to the steady level; the verdict
takes the deviation from Tables 4 and 5 itself. Everything else goes in as
the deviation the clause defines. Clause 16 also carries what shows that its
steps cover the procedure of 16.3: over a linear operating range of 80 dB,
the 25 steps a laboratory takes, which the verdict's figure draws in one
slot. The meter's two level ranges, which no record can count, are declared
beside it; section 5 says why.

```python
from phonometry import metrology

print(result.failed)  # (('18', 'F, 0.25 ms'),)
result.plot()
```

The verdict is an object, not a boolean: `result.passes` is the answer, and
`bool(result)` raises rather than let `if verify_sound_level_meter_periodic(...):`
pass every meter. Each requirement is one `result.requirement(name)`, with
its results under `labels` and one `ConformanceVerification` each under
`verifications`, and `result.requirements` holds them in the order of the
standard.

## 4. Tonebursts and the C-weighted peak

The reference responses the toneburst test is judged from are those of
IEC 61672-1 Table 4, which its NOTES derive from Equation (7) for a maximum
time-weighted level,

$$
\delta_\mathrm{ref} = 10\,\lg\!\left(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-T_\mathrm{b}/\tau}\right)\ \mathrm{dB},
$$

with $\tau$ = 0.125 s for F and 1 s for S, and from Equation (8) for a sound
exposure level, $\delta_\mathrm{ref} = 10\,\lg(T_\mathrm{b}/T_0)$ dB with
$T_0$ = 1 s, both rounded to a tenth of a decibel. Their limits widen as the
toneburst shortens, and the S rows have limits of their own. The module
publishes the table as `IEC61672_TABLE_4`, keyed `"F"`, `"S"` and `"E"`:

```python
row = metrology.IEC61672_TABLE_4["F"][-1]
print(row.duration_ms, row.reference_response_db, row.limits_db(1))
# 0.25 -27.0 (-3.0, 1.0)
worst = result.requirement("toneburst").verifications[2]
print(round(worst.deviation, 2), worst.lower_limit)  # -3.4 -3.0
```

The meter read -30.4 dB for the 0.25 ms toneburst, 3.4 dB below the
reference, where class 1 allows 3.0 dB. Its uncertainty, 0.25 dB, is within
the 0.30 dB of Table B.1, so the result is usable and the meter fails the
clause.

## 5. What a complete test holds

A pass is a verdict on a complete record. 8.1 asks for every test of a
design feature IEC 61672-1 requires and the meter provides. A record shows
the features whose results it holds, and the verdict holds it to them:

- Clauses 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 18 and 21 for every meter;
- frequency weighting A in Clause 13 always, and C as well for class 1,
  which has to provide it (5.1.9, 5.1.10 of IEC 61672-1), and for a meter
  with a C-weighted peak, which 5.1.10 has measure C-weighted time-averaged
  sound level too;
- every weighting any clause names, whether Clause 13, the self-generated
  noise of 11.2 or 14.2, tested in all three (C and Z compared with A in
  14.2);
- Clause 16 over the steps of 16.3: up from the starting point in 5 dB steps
  until within 5 dB of the upper boundary of the linear operating range, in
  1 dB steps from there up to, but not including, the first indication of
  overload, and down the same way to the first indication of under-range. A
  step wider than that, or a record without the levels to tell, leaves the
  clause incomplete; smaller steps cover more and are accepted. An overload
  or under-range indication inside the linear operating range, where the
  manual says the meter shows neither (IEC 61672-1 5.6.10), fails the clause
  (16.4);
- Clause 17 at 5 dB above the first under-range indication on every level
  range, the reference one included and first (17.4 tests "each level
  range"), and with the reference level held on every other one (17.3), NaN
  where a range does not display it. On the reference level range 17.3 has
  nothing to read: the reference level 17.2 set is the anticipated level
  there, a deviation of zero by construction (IEC 61672-1 5.6.3);
- the F, S and time-averaged displays in the toneburst tests of Clause 18,
  the sound exposure level calculated from the time-averaged one when the
  meter does not measure it (18.2), and the other way round: 14.3, with S,
  for a meter whose S-time-weighted toneburst Clause 18 holds, and with the
  time-averaged display for a meter whose overload indication Clause 20
  holds;
- Clause 20 for a meter that displays time-averaged sound level (20.1);
- the environmental conditions within 80 kPa to 105 kPa, 20 °C to 26 °C and
  25 % to 70 % (7.1), recorded at the start and the end at least (7.2).

What a record cannot show is a feature the meter lacks: a meter with one
level range and a record that left Clause 17 out read the same, and so do a
meter without C-weighted peak and a record without Clause 19. The instruction
manual says which optional features the meter has, and
`metrology.SoundLevelMeterFeatures` carries that answer to the verdict,
through `features=`: `True` for a feature the meter has, `False` for one it
lacks, `None` (the default) for one not declared. A feature declared present
owes its tests as a shown one does, and one declared absent takes its
clauses out of the test. A feature neither declared nor shown keeps the
verdict from passing, as the record could be hiding a test left out.

The level ranges are a number, `level_ranges`, because a record cannot count
them either: a Clause 17 with one reading reads the same for a meter with two
level ranges and for a record that left the others out. `level_ranges=1`
takes Clause 17 out; a larger number is how many ranges 17.4 covers, the
reference one included, and 17.3 covers besides it, and until it is
declared, a meter whose Clause 17 shows several keeps the question open. A feature declared absent also takes
away the one IEC 61672-1 makes depend on it: a meter without frequency
weighting C measures no C-weighted peak sound level (5.1.10), so Clause 19 is
not applicable rather than undeclared, and a meter without F has no S
(5.1.9). A declaration without F, a time-averaged display and sound exposure
level is refused, as 5.1.9 asks one of them of every sound level meter.

`missing` names the clauses not measured, `incomplete` what a measured clause
is short of, `undeclared` the features the verdict cannot place,
`not_applicable` the clauses the declared features take out, and
`conditions_outside` the readings outside 7.1. The record of section 3 shows
every feature, and `meter` gives the number of level ranges, so nothing is
open. Without Clauses 17 and 19 it is open on both, until the meter is
declared with one level range and no C-weighted peak:

```python
import dataclasses

basic = dataclasses.replace(
    record,
    range_linearity_deviations_db=None,
    range_linearity_uncertainties_db=None,
    c_peak_differences_db=None,
    c_peak_uncertainties_db=None,
    c_peak_overload_indicated=None,
)
unsaid = metrology.verify_sound_level_meter_periodic(1, basic)
print([name for name, _ in unsaid.undeclared])
# ['level_ranges', 'c_weighted_peak']
declared = metrology.verify_sound_level_meter_periodic(
    1,
    basic,
    features=metrology.SoundLevelMeterFeatures(
        level_ranges=1, c_weighted_peak=False
    ),
)
print(declared.undeclared, [clause for clause, _ in declared.not_applicable])
# () ['17', '19']
```

The verdict's figure gives every graded clause one slot, however many
results it holds, and keeps one for every graded clause the record does not
hold, named under the slot: not measured, not declared or not applicable.

The same record without the S toneburst results, whose meter 14.3 shows to
have S, is incomplete:


So is a Clause 16 of one step at the starting point, however well it
conforms: it covers neither the way up to the overload nor the way down to
the under-range.

```python

```

And so is a Clause 17 whose 17.4 leaves out the reference level range, NaN
in its first place: 17.4 reads "for each level range", and a level 5 dB
above the first under-range indication is not the reference level 17.2 set
there.

```python
nan = float("nan")
no_reference = dataclasses.replace(
    record,
    range_linearity_deviations_db={"17.3": [0.1], "17.4": [nan, -0.2]},
    range_linearity_uncertainties_db={"17.3": [0.18], "17.4": [nan, 0.18]},
)
open_17 = metrology.verify_sound_level_meter_periodic(1, no_reference, features=meter)

# (('17', '17.4 was not measured on the reference level range, and it tests

```

The conditions matter to the uncertainty too: below 97 kPa, and unless the
model's data say otherwise, 7.3 asks the laboratory to add a standard
uncertainty of 0.09 dB up to 3 kHz and 0.14 dB above it to its tests of the
frequency weightings. That component belongs to the laboratory's budget, and
the verdict reads the expanded uncertainty it is given.

## 6. The statement of Clause 22

`result.statement` is the text the documentation of the test carries,
chosen as Clause 22 chooses it and with the class and the year filled in.
The first of these that applies decides it:

- **a notice** when the tests were not performed within the conditions of
  7.1: tests outside them are not periodic tests to IEC 61672-3, whatever
  their results, and none of 22 r), s) and t) is theirs;
- **22 t)** when a result lies outside its limits, a check fails or a
  result exceeds its maximum only by the manufacturer's correction data
  (4.4): the meter
  "did not successfully complete the class 1 tests of IEC 61672-3:2013" and
  "did not conform to the class 1 specifications of IEC 61672-1:2013",
  followed by the tests not completed and why, in the words its NOTE
  suggests;
- **a notice** when a result is unusable (4.3) or the record is
  incomplete, as 22 r) and s) both need the results of all the periodic
  tests;
- **22 r)** when every result passes, evidence of the model's pattern
  approval under IEC 61672-2 is publicly available
  (`pattern_approval_public=True`) and the correction data of the acoustical
  test came from the instruction manual (`corrections_in_manual=True`): the
  meter "conforms to the class 1 specifications of IEC 61672-1:2013";
- **22 s)** when every result passes but either is missing: no general
  conclusion can be drawn about IEC 61672-1, because the periodic tests
  "cover only a limited subset of the specifications".

Both declarations default to `False`: 22 c) and 12.5 have the laboratory
state them, and the verdict assumes neither. With the 0.25 ms toneburst
brought within its limit, a public pattern approval and the correction data
of Clause 12 taken from the instruction manual, as 12.3 asks first, the same
meter earns 22 r). `corrections_in_manual=True` is what says where the data
came from: data from the manufacturer of the microphone or of the calibrator
(12.4), or a source left undeclared, would give 22 s) instead.

```python
fixed = dataclasses.replace(
    record,
    toneburst_responses_db={
        "F": [-1.1, -18.2, -29.6],
        "S": [-7.5, -27.4],
        "E": [-7.0, -27.1, -36.6],
    },
)
again = metrology.verify_sound_level_meter_periodic(
    1,
    fixed,
    pattern_approval_public=True,
    corrections_in_manual=True,
    features=meter,
)
print(again.passes)                                  # True
print(again.statement.endswith("of IEC 61672-1:2013."))  # True
```

A laboratory whose uncertainty at 8 kHz in Clause 12 is 0.85 dB, past the
0.70 dB of Table B.1 only because of the manufacturer's correction data,
gives the 0.66 dB it has without them beside the total. The result is then
one that did not conform, by 4.4, and the statement is 22 t) with the reason
the NOTE to 22 t) gives:

```python
over = dataclasses.replace(
    fixed,

reason = check.requirement("acoustic_weighting").failure_reason("8 kHz")
```

**IEC 61672-3:2013, the grading of a laboratory's results:** the conformance
rule of 4.1 on every graded result of Clauses 12 to 21, with the acceptance
limits of IEC 61672-1:2013 (Table 3, 5.5.9, 5.6.5, 5.8.3, Table 4, 5.11.3,
Table 5, 5.14.2 and 5.15.2) and the maxima of its Table B.1; the unusable
results of 4.3 and the exception of 4.4 for the uncertainty of the
manufacturer's correction data; the yes/no requirements of 16.4, 19.3, 19.5
and 20.5; the
environmental conditions of 7.1 and 7.2; the completeness 8.1 asks for, from
the features the record shows and those `SoundLevelMeterFeatures` declares
present or absent, with what IEC 61672-1 5.1.9 and 5.1.10 make of a feature
declared absent; the extent of Clause 16 over the steps of 16.3 and of
Clause 17 over every level range (17.3, 17.4); and the statements of 22 r),
s) and t). Tables 4, 5 and B.1 of IEC 61672-1 are published read-only.
</ScopeClaim>

<ScopeClaim status="not-covered" label="Not covered">
The tests themselves: the library drives no calibrator, coupler, actuator or
electrical input. The level linearity deviations of 16.2 (by the procedure of
IEC 61672-2), the relative frequency weightings of 12.15 and 13.9 with their
corrections, and the laboratory's uncertainty budget are inputs. The checks
that are not a measured deviation are the laboratory's record: the instruction
manual and markings of Clause 3, the preliminary inspection of Clause 5, the
power supply of Clause 6, the calibrator's own conformance of Clause 9 (which
`metrology.verify_sound_calibrator` grades against IEC 60942), the general
test requirements of 8.2 to 8.5, among them the confirmation of 8.4 that an
electrical output used for the tests reads as the display does (within the
±0.1 dB of IEC 61672-1 5.19.4), and the source of the correction data of
12.2 to 12.6. The 22 l) statement, which the documentation carries when the
free-field correction data come without an uncertainty, is not written.
</ScopeClaim>
